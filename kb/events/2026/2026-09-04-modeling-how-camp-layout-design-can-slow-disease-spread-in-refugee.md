Researchers Giulia Zarpellon, Joseph Aylett-Bullock, Frank Krauss and Miguel Luengo-Oroz released a paper on arXiv presenting a methodology to study how the physical layout of refugee and internally displaced person (IDP) camps can influence disease spread[^arxiv-2609.05342]. The work uses the agent-based JUNE epidemic model to run proof-of-concept experiments testing different camp layout scenarios as mitigation strategies. The authors argue that layout design choices affect how people move and interact within camps, which in turn shapes transmission dynamics. They frame the methodology as a basis for future investigations that could inform operational planning for humanitarian settlements, with the paper linked to a 2023 ICLR workshop on machine learning and global health.
