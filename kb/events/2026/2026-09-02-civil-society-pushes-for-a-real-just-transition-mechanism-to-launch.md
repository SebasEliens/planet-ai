Civil society groups that helped secure last year's COP30 decision in Belém to establish a Just Transition Mechanism are now pushing for it to be operationalised at COP31 in Antalya this November[^src1]. Mid-year talks in Bonn produced an informal document outlining possible design options for the mechanism, but countries remain divided on whether its scope should focus narrowly on phasing out fossil fuels or take a broader 'whole of economy' approach[^src1]. Governments also disagree on whether the review of the existing Just Transition Work Programme should be linked to or kept separate from the mechanism's launch, with Russia, Arab states and some large emerging economies favouring separation and the African Group backing joint work[^src1]. Negotiators such as Camila Mercure of FARN stressed that the mechanism should enable international cooperation, guide national just transition strategies, and act as a channel for accessing funding[^src1]. An informal workshop for governments and NGOs on the mechanism was scheduled for late September in Sydney ahead of further debate at COP31[^src1].
