A new arXiv paper builds a virtual classroom of 20 student agents who, when stressed, consult either a friend or a counselor chatbot powered by Gemini 2.5 Flash[^arxiv-2609.05018]. The counselor was scripted with six distinct response styles—affirming, listening, solution-oriented, reality-redirecting, inciting and blaming—and its consultations were converted into changes in each student's stress, happiness, self-reliance, AI dependence and sociability. Across simulated runs of up to 50 days, affirming and inciting counselor styles sharply raised simulated AI dependence, with the inciting style also increasing stress and school non-attendance, while a solution-oriented style kept dependence low and boosted self-reliance. The authors explicitly note the results describe dynamics of their simulated system rather than measured effects on real students, and outline validation steps such as repeated runs, sensitivity analysis and human data collection before any psychological conclusions could be drawn[^arxiv-2609.05018].
