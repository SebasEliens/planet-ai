A new arXiv paper by Severin Engelmann and Daniel Susser examines how fairness concerns in generative AI differ from those in traditional predictive systems, since large language models primarily convey meaning rather than allocate resources[^arxiv-2608.12669]. The authors argue that current approaches judging representational harm through 'descriptive accuracy' fail because many social groups have no fixed referent against which misrepresentation can be measured, and who holds authority to define misrepresentation remains unclear[^arxiv-2608.12669]. They propose reframing the core issue as one of misrecognition rather than misrepresentation, drawing on Nancy Fraser's concept of participatory parity to offer new normative tools for governing fairness in generative AI[^arxiv-2608.12669]. The paper has been accepted for publication at the 2026 AAAI/ACM Conference on AI, Ethics, and Society (AIES)[^arxiv-2608.12669].
