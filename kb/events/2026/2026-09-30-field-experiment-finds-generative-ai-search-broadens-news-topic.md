Researchers ran a randomized field experiment with 37,561 readers of The Washington Post, giving a treatment group AI-generated answers with article citations displayed above standard search results while a control group saw only conventional results[^arxiv-2609.38946]. The study found that AI answers increased reach of popular topics but also shifted overall consumption toward less-popular topics, both for individual readers and across the full audience[^arxiv-2609.38946]. Cited articles linked from AI answers were a major driver of this diversification, while AI answers themselves accounted for most of the rise in shared information consumed without requiring article clicks[^arxiv-2609.38946]. The authors conclude that generative AI search can simultaneously diversify collective attention and increase the amount of information readers have in common, countering fears that such tools inevitably create narrower filter bubbles[^arxiv-2609.38946].
