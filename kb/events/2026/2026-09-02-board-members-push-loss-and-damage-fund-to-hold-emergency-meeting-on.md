Following flash flooding in Nepal that killed hundreds and caused an estimated $2.56 billion in damage, eight developing-country board members of the UN's Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage signed a letter calling for an emergency board meeting[^src1]. The letter argues the disaster, linked to a glacial slope collapse after unusually hot weather, is exactly the type of climate extreme the fund was designed to address[^src1]. Civil society campaigner Harjeet Singh said climate disasters "cannot wait for scheduled committee cycles" and urged the board co-chairs to convene an emergency session[^src1]. The fund, which has yet to approve any funding requests since its first call for proposals last year, is limited to $20 million per project in its current phase and has allocated roughly $350 million of the $820 million pledged by wealthy nations[^src1].
