An AlgorithmWatch investigation reveals that the Dutch National Police discontinued their Crime Anticipation System (CAS) in late 2025/early 2026 after a decade of use without ever proving it reduced crime[^src1]. A confidential internal 'Kraai' quality report from September 2025, released through freedom-of-information requests, found that only one in fifty incidents in Amsterdam was correctly predicted by the system, far below the accuracy figures police had cited when it launched[^src1]. A police spokesperson admitted the tool was dropped because officers could not demonstrate that burglaries actually decreased due to its use[^src1]. CAS had divided cities into small grid cells and combined crime history with socioeconomic data such as income, family size, and benefit recipiency to flag 'hotspot' areas for patrol, a practice a 2023 Human Rights and AI Impact Assessment found risked violating rights to equal treatment, presumption of innocence, and explainability[^src1].
