In April 2024, the Biden EPA finalized the HON rule, requiring about 220 U.S. petrochemical plants to reduce emissions of six hazardous chemicals and install fenceline monitoring, a measure the agency projected would cut cancer risk near affected plants by 96 percent[^grist-hon-rule-2026]. Communities in Louisiana's 'Cancer Alley,' which has the nation's highest air-pollution cancer risk and disproportionately affects Black residents, had pushed for the rule for years[^grist-hon-rule-2026]. Since early 2025 the Trump administration has granted two-year exemptions to more than 50 facilities and added 20 more in July 2026, while the EPA says it will propose a weakened rewrite of the rule this fall[^grist-hon-rule-2026]. The HON rule is one of nine Biden-era air toxics regulations now being rescinded, revised, or reconsidered under the current administration[^grist-hon-rule-2026].
