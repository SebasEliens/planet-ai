At the pre-COP31 summit held in Fiji on 6 October 2026, Pacific leaders and partner officials criticised the global climate finance system for being slow, debt-heavy and bureaucratically burdensome, after a visit to Tuvalu's Funafuti atoll to see sea-defence construction firsthand[^src1]. Palau's President Surangel Whipps Jr. compared the region's situation to a sinking canoe handed a patch wrapped in red tape, while Solomon Islands PM Matthew Wale described losing family homes to rising seas[^src1]. Leaders renewed calls for donors to invest directly in the Pacific Resilience Facility, a Pacific Islands Forum-designed fund seeking $500 million in pledges by COP31, which has so far secured about $180 million without new commitments from the UN General Assembly[^src1]. The gathering, unusually elevated this year with a dedicated 'leaders segment' led by COP31 president Australia, drew only a small number of non-Pacific heads of state alongside officials from the Green Climate Fund and Asian Development Bank[^src1].
