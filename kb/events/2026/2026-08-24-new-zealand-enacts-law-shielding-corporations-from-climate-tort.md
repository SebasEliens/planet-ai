New Zealand's parliament adopted the Climate Change Response (Tort Liability) Amendment Bill on 2026-08-24, blocking all present and future civil tort claims over emissions-related climate harm[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. Justice minister Paul Goldsmith said the law gives businesses 'certainty around their climate change obligations' and keeps climate policy at the national level rather than in courts[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. The bill halts Mike Smith's long-running lawsuit against Fonterra, Genesis Energy, New Zealand Steel and other major emitters, which had a trial scheduled for April 2027 after the Supreme Court unanimously allowed it to proceed[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. The law passed 67-53 despite widespread opposition submissions and criticism that it followed lobbying by the defendant companies, and Smith has filed a complaint with the UN special rapporteur on climate change and human rights while separately challenging the law in court[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill].
