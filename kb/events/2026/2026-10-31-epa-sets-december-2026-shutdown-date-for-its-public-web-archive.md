The EPA quietly changed a banner on its public web archive to set a decommissioning date of December 31, 2026, which would remove public access to tens of thousands of environmental documents dating back to the 1990s[^grist-epa-archive]. This is the third attempt to shutter the archive, after plans in 2022 and 2023 were delayed following backlash from historians, librarians, and transparency groups such as EDGI[^grist-epa-archive]. EDGI co-founder Gretchen Gehrke warned that the archive's loss would be irreversible, noting it has served as a tool for "democratic oversight" for two decades[^grist-epa-archive]. The archive has documented major regulatory shifts, including the Clear Skies Initiative and Clean Power Plan, and preserved climate pages deleted during Trump's first term, as well as records tied to the Camp Lejeune contamination case in North Carolina[^grist-epa-archive]. Freedom of the Press Foundation's Lauren Harper said eliminating easy access to the archive would force researchers and lawmakers to rely more heavily on an overloaded FOIA system[^grist-epa-archive].
