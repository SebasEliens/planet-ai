Ahead of the 2026-27 school year, New York City and Los Angeles enacted new restrictions on classroom AI use, reversing the more permissive stance both districts took after briefly banning ChatGPT in 2022-23[^techpolicy-press-2026-09-05]. NYC's revised policy bans student-facing AI in K-8 classrooms while permitting a limited set of approved tools for high schoolers, tightening an earlier proposal that had assigned most uses a 'yellow light' risk category[^techpolicy-press-2026-09-05]. LAUSD separately announced a one-year moratorium on generative AI use on district devices covering roughly 378,000 students, a decision officials said caught even some school staff by surprise[^techpolicy-press-2026-09-05]. Both actions followed sustained organizing by parent, teacher, and student coalitions, including the AIM Coalition in NYC and Schools Beyond Screens in LA, who welcomed the moves as a step toward stronger safeguards[^techpolicy-press-2026-09-05]. Officials in both districts now have a year to develop longer-term AI policies.
