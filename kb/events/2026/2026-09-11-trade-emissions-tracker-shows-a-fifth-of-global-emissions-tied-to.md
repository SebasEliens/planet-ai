A newly launched trade emissions tracker, produced with analysis from the European Climate Foundation and consultancy Matière, finds that about a fifth of global greenhouse gas emissions are tied to internationally traded goods and services[^climatehome-2026-09-15]. The report highlights that many importing countries do not count these emissions in their national inventories, even though in places like Austria and Sweden imported emissions can rival the country's entire domestic carbon footprint[^climatehome-2026-09-15]. China is shown as both the leading exporter and importer of trade-linked emissions, with the US a close second at around 1.6 billion tonnes of CO2 equivalent in imports[^climatehome-2026-09-15]. Former EU climate negotiator Jacob Werksman said addressing this gap requires international agreement on how to measure carbon content in traded products[^climatehome-2026-09-15]. The findings come as trade and climate emerged as a new discussion topic at UN climate talks this year, though it remains outside formal negotiations ahead of COP31 in Antalya[^climatehome-2026-09-15].
