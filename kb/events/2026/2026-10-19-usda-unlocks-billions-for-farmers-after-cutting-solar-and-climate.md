The USDA, after earlier cutting solar eligibility from a renewable-energy grant program and removing climate-related webpages, announced an $8 billion diesel relief program on October 19 to offset farmers' fuel cost increases tied partly to the Iran war's supply disruptions[^grist-usda-farmers-2026]. On October 26 it added $57 million to help independent ranchers expand meat-processing capacity, and around October 30 it announced nearly $500 million in commodity purchases from U.S. producers[^grist-usda-farmers-2026]. These moves arrive as farmers face record beef prices, drought-reduced herds, and a stalled farm bill, with critics like the National Sustainable Agriculture Coalition calling the payments insufficient given rising farm bankruptcies and debt[^grist-usda-farmers-2026]. The timing, days before the midterm elections, follows prior USDA actions widely seen as reducing support for climate adaptation and renewable energy among agricultural producers[^grist-usda-farmers-2026].
