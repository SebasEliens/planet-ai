Following catastrophic flash floods triggered by a rock-ice avalanche in Nepal on 26 August 2026 that killed over 1,400 people, the Nepali government requested $20m in financial compensation from the UN fund for responding to loss and damage[^carbonbrief-nepal-loss-damage]. Carbon Brief's analysis found this sum covers just 0.7% of the $2.7bn in physical damage and economic losses Nepal's disaster authority estimated, falling to 0.3% once $4.8bn in recovery and reconstruction needs are factored in[^carbonbrief-nepal-loss-damage]. The request, capped at the fund's $20m per-project limit, makes Nepal the fund's fourth-largest claimant among 176 applications from 118 countries totalling $2.8bn, against only $822m pledged mostly by developed nations and no disbursements made since the fund's 2023 creation[^carbonbrief-nepal-loss-damage]. Nepali officials and board members from Asia-Pacific and African states pushed for a rapid emergency allocation, but developed-country board members only agreed to a "consultation" without funding commitments, prompting criticism from loss-and-damage campaigner Harjeet Singh that developed countries gave a "really cold response" to Nepal[^carbonbrief-nepal-loss-damage].
