Governments concluded the COP17 UN biodiversity summit in Yerevan, Armenia on October 31, 2026, adopting a decision dubbed the 'Yerevan Pathway' after a near-collapse over financing disputes[^ccn-cop17]. The deal invites large emerging economies like China and Gulf states to voluntarily assume donor responsibilities for biodiversity funding alongside developed nations, a first for the Convention on Biological Diversity process[^ccn-cop17]. It also calls on countries to update national biodiversity plans and accelerate cuts to subsidies harming nature, responding to a UN progress report showing the world off track on 22 of 23 global targets, including protecting 30% of land and sea by 2030[^ccn-cop17]. Civil society and country groups, including Pacific Island delegations and Greenpeace, welcomed progress but warned that unresolved finance gaps and low replenishment of the Global Environment Facility threaten implementation[^ccn-cop17].
