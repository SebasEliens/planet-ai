Grist reports that Brazil's October 25 presidential runoff between Lula da Silva and Flávio Bolsonaro is being treated by environmental scientists and economists as a decisive moment for Amazon deforestation policy[^grist-brazil-amazon-election-2026]. Climate scientist Carlos Nobre warned that a Bolsonaro victory would likely increase deforestation, while economist Monica de Bolle noted that the elder Bolsonaro's term saw enforcement collapse and emissions nearly double. The article cites MapBiomas data finding 97 percent of 2025 Amazon clearing showed signs of illegality despite legal limits in the Forest Code, and references a May 2026 Nature study led by Nico Wunderling placing the Amazon's dieback tipping point at 22 to 28 percent total clearing combined with projected mid-century warming. Lula has pledged net-zero deforestation by 2030, while under his current term clearing rates have fallen annually, contrasted with projections that a return to prior Bolsonaro-era policy could clear roughly double the forest area over the next four years.
