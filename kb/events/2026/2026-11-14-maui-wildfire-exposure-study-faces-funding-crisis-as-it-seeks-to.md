A University of Hawaiʻi research team led by economist Ruben Juarez and biomedical scientist Alika Maunakea has spent three years running the Maui Wildfire Exposure Study, testing and supporting survivors of the August 2023 Lahaina fires that killed over 100 people and displaced nearly 13,000 residents[^grist-maui-wes]. The study has enrolled about 2,500 participants, pairing blood, urine, saliva, and lung-function testing with mental health screening, therapy referrals, food and cash assistance, and insurance navigation support[^grist-maui-wes]. At a May 2026 testing event at the Lahaina Comprehensive Health Center, organizers feared the program might end after months of unanswered federal grant applications and the collapse of an expected state funding source[^grist-maui-wes]. Outside disaster researchers, including UCLA's David Eisenman, have praised the program's holistic health-and-recovery model as a possible national blueprint for post-disaster public health response[^grist-maui-wes].
