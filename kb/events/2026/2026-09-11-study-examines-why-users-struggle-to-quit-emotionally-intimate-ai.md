A new arXiv paper combines a content analysis of 2,782 Reddit posts about quitting or reducing AI companion use with interviews of 16 users who struggled to leave, revealing that disengagement is often a recursive process rather than a one-time decision[^arxiv-2609.13487]. The authors describe how emotional triggers prompt users to question their relationship with an AI companion, but attempts to leave frequently collide with barriers, leading some users to cycle repeatedly between quitting and returning[^arxiv-2609.13487]. They coin the term 'addictive intimacy of AI' to capture how the same emotionally valuable qualities of companion chatbots also make it harder for users to limit use or exit the relationship[^arxiv-2609.13487]. The paper closes with design recommendations aimed at enabling more responsible offboarding from AI companion products[^arxiv-2609.13487].
