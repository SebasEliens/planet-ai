Carbon Brief's analysis finds that Nepal's $20m request to the UN loss-and-damage fund, submitted after flash floods on 26 August killed more than 1,400 people, covers only 0.7% of the $2.7bn in damage and losses the government has estimated, or 0.3% when $4.8bn in recovery and reconstruction costs are added[^1]. The $20m sum is the maximum allowed per project and still makes Nepal the fourth-largest claimant among 176 requests totalling $2.8bn filed by 118 developing countries since the fund opened, versus only $822m pledged mostly by developed nations and no payouts made to date[^1]. Nepal's finance minister asked the fund's board for an 'urgent response' to demonstrate the fund could act with speed and solidarity, and board members from Asia-Pacific and African states urged a provisional rapid-response allocation, but developed-country members only agreed on 14 September to a consultation without committing funds[^1]. Nepal plans to bolster its case using climate attribution science linking glacial retreat and permafrost thaw to the disaster, with a full attribution study still pending as of the report[^1].
