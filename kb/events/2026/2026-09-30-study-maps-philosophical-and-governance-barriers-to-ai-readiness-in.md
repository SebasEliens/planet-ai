A new arXiv paper develops a philosophical framework for assessing whether AI-based disease surveillance tools are actually ready for deployment in low- and middle-income countries[^arxiv-2609.39513]. The authors argue that most prior research has focused on prediction accuracy while neglecting structural and normative conditions such as fragmented health information systems, digital inequality, governance gaps, and weak institutional capacity[^arxiv-2609.39513]. Through qualitative thematic and philosophical analysis, they identify four interlocking readiness dimensions: epistemic adequacy, distributive justice, ethics of governance, and institutional legitimacy[^arxiv-2609.39513]. The paper frames AI implementation in public health surveillance as a combined socio-technical and normative process, intended to guide more context-sensitive and institutionally sustainable system design in resource-constrained settings[^arxiv-2609.39513].
