Leaders from the 18-member Pacific Islands Forum met in Koror, Palau, for a week-long summit demanding that COP31 in Türkiye deliver climate finance, renewable energy support and ocean protection commitments[^src1]. The forum's final declaration called climate change the single greatest threat to Pacific peoples' security and wellbeing, and endorsed the Belau Declaration urging countries to keep the 1.5C goal alive and accelerate the shift away from fossil fuels[^src1]. Leaders pointed to a $14-billion '100% Renewable Blue Pacific' investment plan, including a $52-million Australian-run off-grid renewables programme and a $100-million blended finance fund for wind and solar, meant to cut the region's heavy reliance on costly diesel imports[^src1]. Australia's climate minister Chris Bowen said his government intends to use its COP31 presidency role to make climate finance more accessible and to seek global support for the Pacific Resilience Facility, which channels funds to climate-vulnerable communities[^src1]. The summit also saw preparations for a pre-COP31 political gathering in Fiji and Tuvalu in October, aimed at building momentum for strong outcomes at the November talks[^src1].
