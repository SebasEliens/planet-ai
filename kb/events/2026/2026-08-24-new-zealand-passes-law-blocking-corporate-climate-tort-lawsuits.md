New Zealand's parliament adopted the Climate Change Response (Tort Liability) Amendment Bill on 2026-08-24, barring civil tort claims—past, present and future—over emissions-related climate harm against any person or entity, including the government[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. The law directly shuts down Mike Smith's landmark suit against six companies, which sought court orders to halt emissions rather than compensation and had a trial set for April 2027[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. Justice minister Paul Goldsmith said the change gives businesses "certainty around their climate change obligations" and keeps climate policy at the national rather than judicial level[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. The bill passed its third reading 67-53 despite widespread opposition in public submissions, and follows revealed lobbying by the defendant companies plus a finding that the Prime Minister's Office withheld information about contacts on the case[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill]. Smith, alongside Māori and youth groups, has filed a UN complaint and a fresh legal challenge against the new law[^climatechangenews-nz-tort-bill].
