Indonesian wildfires burning through 2026's El Niño-driven dry season have released 76 million tonnes of carbon as of 7 September, putting the year on a trajectory similar to the catastrophic 2015 season that produced 333MtC[^carbonbrief-indonesia-fires-2026]. Wageningen University researcher Guido van der Werf said fires are "more or less on track" to match 2015 levels, with areas like Papua burning more intensely than ever recorded[^carbonbrief-indonesia-fires-2026]. Experts including Copernicus's Mark Parrington and Yayasan Konservasi Alam Nusantara's Nisa Novita attribute the scale to El Niño amplifying dry, degraded peatland conditions created by agricultural drainage and land clearance[^carbonbrief-indonesia-fires-2026]. The article notes uncertainty over whether Indonesia's peatland restoration policies since 2015 have meaningfully reduced fire risk, especially after the country's dedicated peatland restoration agency was dissolved last year[^carbonbrief-indonesia-fires-2026].
