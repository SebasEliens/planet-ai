Eight developing-country board members of the UN's Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage sent a letter calling for an emergency board meeting to allocate money for Nepal after catastrophic flash flooding killed hundreds and caused an estimated $2.56 billion in damage[^climatechangenews-2026-09-02]. The letter argues the flooding, believed to have been triggered by a glacial slope collapse amid unusually hot weather, is precisely the kind of climate disaster the fund was designed to address[^climatechangenews-2026-09-02]. Despite having received 180 project proposals and $820 million in pledges, the fund has yet to approve any funding and remains capped at $20 million per project during its initial phase, with only $350 million allocated so far[^climatechangenews-2026-09-02]. Civil society advocate Harjeet Singh urged the fund's co-chairs to convene immediately, saying "the Loss and Damage Fund was built for moments exactly like this"[^climatechangenews-2026-09-02].
