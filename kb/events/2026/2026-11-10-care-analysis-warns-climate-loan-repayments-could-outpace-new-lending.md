A new CARE Denmark and CARE Climate Justice Center analysis of about 17,500 climate loan records from 2011-2024 finds that repayments on public climate loans, including interest and fees, nearly tripled from $6.9 billion in 2020 to $20.1 billion in 2024[^climatechangenews-2026-11-10]. Researchers project that if lending continues at 2024 levels and terms, annual repayments could overtake new climate lending by 2036, with the $489 billion loan portfolio expected to cost borrowers roughly $663 billion over its lifetime[^climatechangenews-2026-11-10]. CARE senior adviser John Nordbo said rich countries risk masking the true scale of support, noting the reliance on loans "doesn't really seem sustainable"[^climatechangenews-2026-11-10]. The findings come as developing countries face a record $1.4 trillion in debt repayments in 2025 per the World Bank, and as ActionAid reports climate-vulnerable nations spend nearly 25 times more on debt than climate action, intensifying debate over climate finance ahead of COP31[^climatechangenews-2026-11-10].
