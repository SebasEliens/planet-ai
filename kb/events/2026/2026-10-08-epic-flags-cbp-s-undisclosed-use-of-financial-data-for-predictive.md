EPIC's analysis, drawing on a 404 Media investigation, describes how CBP's Predictive Intelligence Targeting Teams (PITT) have been using financial transaction data to identify people exhibiting 'patterns commonly associated with illicit narcotics activity' and then funneling those leads to local police to obscure CBP's involvement[^epic-cbp-pitt-financial-data]. CBP has declined to explain how it obtained the underlying financial records or whether it secured a warrant, as illustrated by the case of Kyle Olson, who was pulled over in Montana this May under a pretextual traffic stop after being flagged by a CBP unit[^epic-cbp-pitt-financial-data]. The piece links this to earlier incidents, including CBP's use of license-plate readers to flag Alek Schott's travel route in 2022 and the arrest of truck driver Lorenzo Gutierrez Lugo, who spent about $20,000 clearing his name[^epic-cbp-pitt-financial-data]. It also notes that ICE separately used administrative subpoenas between 2019 and 2021 to bulk-collect 6.2 million financial records from Western Union and Maxitransfers, a program reportedly ended after criticism from Senator Ron Wyden[^epic-cbp-pitt-financial-data]. EPIC argues these practices exemplify predictive policing's reliance on unreliable, bias-prone data and calls on lawmakers to restrict federal agencies' use of financial and travel surveillance for such targeting[^epic-cbp-pitt-financial-data].
