A Markup investigation examined how hospitals and the widely used MyChart patient portal, made by Epic Systems, handle patient requests to opt out of sharing health data for purposes beyond direct care[^src1]. The reporter, after registering at 25 clinics and hospitals in seven states, found consent forms routinely bundle data sharing through Epic's Care Everywhere network, regional exchanges like CliniSync, and third-party intake vendors like Phreesia, often mislabeling optional authorizations as mandatory[^src1]. At one University Hospitals facility in Ohio, attempting to opt out of the hospital's claim to own and use any photos or recordings taken of the patient required 26 steps and a notarized document, yet staff still told the reporter opting out wasn't allowed, contradicting the hospital's own written policy[^src1]. The investigation also describes a prior case where Phreesia took 81 days to acknowledge a patient had never actually consented to having her data used for marketing, despite its form saying consent was 'Required'[^src1].
