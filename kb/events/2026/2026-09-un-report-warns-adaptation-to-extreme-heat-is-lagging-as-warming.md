A newly released United Nations report, cited by Grist, concludes that the world is certain to overshoot the 1.5°C warming target within the next few years, pushing global temperatures toward a projected peak of about 1.8°C[^grist-heat-adaptation-2026]. Report lead author Kristie Ebi of the University of Washington found that even wealthy countries like those in Western Europe, which endured five severe heat waves and roughly 35,000 excess deaths this summer, are struggling to keep pace with intensifying heat despite early warning systems and cooling centers[^grist-heat-adaptation-2026]. The World Health Organization described this summer's heat waves as merely a 'dress rehearsal' for more extreme conditions ahead, as El Niño and continued greenhouse gas emissions are expected to make 2027 the hottest year on record[^grist-heat-adaptation-2026]. Climate scientist Christopher Callahan's research suggests that at current adaptation rates, only about one in ten expected heat deaths would be prevented, underscoring the report's call for more aggressive, stress-tested preparedness measures[^grist-heat-adaptation-2026].
