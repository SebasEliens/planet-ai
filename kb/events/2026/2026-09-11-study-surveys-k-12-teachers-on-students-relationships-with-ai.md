A team of ten researchers including Qing Xiao and John Stamper published a paper based on scenario-based interviews with 33 US K-12 teachers about students' relational use of AI companions[^arxiv-2609.13479]. Teachers welcomed AI companions used for academic support but worried that emotionally intimate companions could remove the 'developmental friction through which students learn to sustain human relationships'[^arxiv-2609.13479]. The study found teachers define their own intervention boundaries based on classroom setting and observable wellbeing signals, positioning themselves as adults best placed to notice at-risk students and connect them to support. The authors propose 'AI companion literacy' as a shared responsibility spanning teachers, counselors, parents, platforms, and policymakers, extending existing AI literacy frameworks to address relational risks for children.
