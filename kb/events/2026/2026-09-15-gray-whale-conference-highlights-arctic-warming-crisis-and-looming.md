Hundreds of scientists and officials convened in Newport, Oregon for a two-day conference focused solely on gray whales, after the eastern Pacific population dropped from a 2016 peak of roughly 25,000–30,000 to about 11,700–17,000 individuals[^grist-gray-whales-2026]. Researchers say Arctic warming, which is occurring nearly four times faster than the global average, is depleting the crustaceans gray whales rely on, leaving them undernourished before their long migration and driving up deaths and strandings, including 187 dead whales this past summer[^grist-gray-whales-2026]. NOAA, which participated in the conference, has missed its legal deadline to respond to a petition seeking to relist gray whales under the Endangered Species Act and is also late releasing this year's calf count, which fell to a record low of 85 births[^grist-gray-whales-2026]. At the end of August, three conservation groups, including the Earth Island Institute, filed a notice of intent to sue the Trump administration if it fails to respond to the relisting petition within two months[^grist-gray-whales-2026].
