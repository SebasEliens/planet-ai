A new arXiv entry titled "Warfare" by Nathan Reitinger provides an overview of lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS), defined in international law as weapons that, once switched on, select and engage targets without human intervention[^arxiv-2609.13176]. The paper discusses accountability gaps, the tension between such systems and traditional just war doctrine, and the moral difficulties of letting machines decide whether humans live or die. It aims to break down these issues along both technical and legal lines, positioning itself as a reference-style overview of the current debate over restrictions on autonomous lethal AI[^arxiv-2609.13176].
