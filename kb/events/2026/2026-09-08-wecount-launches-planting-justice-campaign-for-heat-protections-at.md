On the Tuesday after Labor Day 2026, activists with the labor group WeCount delivered petitions to Costa Farms nurseries in Leicester, North Carolina and Apopka, Florida, launching a new campaign called Planting Justice[^grist-2026-09-11]. The campaign asks Costa Farms, the world's largest indoor houseplant grower, to adopt a worker-drafted code of conduct guaranteeing water, shade, rest breaks, and heat-illness training across its five U.S. nursery locations[^grist-2026-09-11]. WeCount, which previously pushed for a Miami-Dade heat standard before Florida preempted local heat rules in 2024, is modeling the effort on the Fair Food Program pioneered by the Coalition of Immokalee Workers[^grist-2026-09-11]. Organizers cite federal data showing agricultural workers, including nursery staff, are over 35 times more likely to die from heat-related illness than other workers, while a stalled federal OSHA heat rule leaves them largely unprotected[^grist-2026-09-11]. Costa Farms responded that its existing heat safety policy, including on-site medical staff and hydration stations, already aligns with proposed OSHA standards[^grist-2026-09-11].
