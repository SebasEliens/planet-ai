The UN reported that since Guterres launched the Early Warning Systems for All goal in 2022, the share of countries with a multi-hazard early warning system has grown from 113 to 136, but roughly 60 nations remain uncovered with the 2027 deadline approaching[^climatechangenews-2026-09-23]. Guterres said "too many people are still left exposed," with the largest gaps concentrated among least developed countries, small island states and fragile or conflict-affected nations[^climatechangenews-2026-09-23]. UN officials highlighted that having a warning system is not enough on its own—Nepali negotiator Manjeet Dhakal described how text alerts about a deadly glacial flood failed to convey the disaster's true scale, echoing failures seen in Spain's 2024 Valencia floods[^climatechangenews-2026-09-23]. UNDP's Rohini Kohli said developing countries also lack foundational climate data and adequate finance, noting adaptation needs exceed $300 billion a year while funding remains far below that level[^climatechangenews-2026-09-23].
