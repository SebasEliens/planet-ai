Leaders from the 18-member Pacific Islands Forum met for a week in Koror, Palau, to press for COP31 to deliver climate finance, renewable energy support and ocean protection commitments for the region[^src1]. The summit's final declaration reaffirmed climate change as the top threat to Pacific peoples and called for a strong political statement at the pre-COP31 gathering in Fiji and Tuvalu in October[^src1]. Leaders also endorsed the Belau Declaration, which insists the 1.5C Paris goal remains achievable and urges accelerated movement away from fossil fuels toward renewables[^src1]. The forum highlighted a $14-billion "100% Renewable Blue Pacific" investment plan, including a $52-million Australian-run off-grid renewables programme and a $100-million blended finance fund, alongside the Pacific Resilience Facility meant to speed disbursement of climate funds to vulnerable communities[^src1]. Australia's COP31 negotiations president Chris Bowen said he would promote the facility and push to make climate finance more accessible for developing countries[^src1].
