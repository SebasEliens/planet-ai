Grist and IndigiNews report on BearSafe Planning, a program founded in 2005 by Inuk elder Derrick Pottle to certify bear guards in Nunatsiavut, northern Labrador[^grist-bear-guards]. The article links rising polar bear encounters to climate-driven changes such as longer ice-free seasons and altered bear habitat, which are pushing bears into areas and seasons where they were once rare[^grist-bear-guards]. Pottle describes the training's goal as providing 'safety, protection, confidence in bear country,' combining Inuit traditional knowledge with formal safety instruction for hunters, families, park staff, and tourism operators[^grist-bear-guards]. The piece highlights how certified guards, including Sherri Wolfrey of Tikigâksuagusik, use the training both for personal safety on the land and as a source of employment in national parks and cruise tourism across Inuit Nunangat[^grist-bear-guards].
