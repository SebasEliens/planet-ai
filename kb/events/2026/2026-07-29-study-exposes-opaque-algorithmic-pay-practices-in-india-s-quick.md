A new arXiv paper examines how India's fast-growing quick commerce sector—dominated by platforms like Blinkit, Zepto, and Swiggy Instamart—relies on opaque algorithms to determine gig delivery workers' pay, task allocation, and performance evaluation[^arxiv-2609.13178]. To address the resulting information gap, the researchers piloted GigSaathi, a chatbot that lets workers submit earnings screenshots, which are converted into time-series wage data, and combined this with incentive data gathered directly from Blinkit stores in New Delhi[^arxiv-2609.13178]. The study found substantial variability in base pay, incentives, hours, and distances travelled, and noted that Blinkit guarantees no minimum base pay per kilometre[^arxiv-2609.13178]. Earnings showed little correlation with time spent per order, suggesting workers are not compensated proportionately for delivery time, while incentives—averaging roughly a quarter of weekly earnings—appeared to function as a labor control mechanism pushing workers to chase unpredictable bonuses[^arxiv-2609.13178].
