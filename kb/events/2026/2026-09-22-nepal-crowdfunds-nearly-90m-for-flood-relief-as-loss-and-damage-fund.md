A government-run crowdfunding campaign, the Prime Minister's Disaster Relief Fund, has collected nearly $90 million from individuals and companies worldwide to help Nepal recover from a devastating Himalayan flood, according to climate negotiator Manjeet Dhakal[^1]. Meanwhile, the UN's Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage has not yet acted on Nepal's August 31 request for $20 million, despite urgent appeals from Nepali ministers and developing-country board members, with its next formal meeting not scheduled until December 15[^1]. UN Secretary-General António Guterres told the UN General Assembly that rich countries' contributions to the FRLD were 'ridiculously small' and urged them to fund it at scale[^1]. Nepal's government estimates total recovery and reconstruction costs at $4.8 billion after the flood killed roughly 1,450 people and left more than 5,000 missing, with a World Weather Attribution study linking the triggering rock-ice avalanche to climate change[^1].
