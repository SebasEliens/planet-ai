The Fund for Responding to Loss and Damage (FRLD) board is set to vote at its first-ever extraordinary meeting on October 22, 2026 on a motion to provisionally set aside $20 million for Nepal to address damage from the catastrophic August 2026 glacial flood[^climatechangenews-2026-10-16]. The disaster killed more than 1,400 people and destroyed homes, roads, bridges and hydropower infrastructure in Nepal and Chinese-administered Tibet[^climatechangenews-2026-10-16]. If approved, this would be the first time the FRLD, agreed at COP27 and operationalized at COP28, allocates funds for a specific disaster rather than through its standard proposal call process[^climatechangenews-2026-10-16]. Campaign group Fill the Fund called the draft decision "a real step forward" but noted the sum is tiny compared with Nepal's estimated $4.8 billion recovery costs and criticized the roughly two-month delay in organizing the emergency meeting[^climatechangenews-2026-10-16]. The fund currently holds only $350 million against $2.8 billion in pending requests from about 180 projects, a gap UN Secretary-General António Guterres has called "ridiculously small"[^climatechangenews-2026-10-16].
