Four days of closed-door talks at the International Maritime Organization in London ended with the IMO describing 'constructive discussions' and 'genuine willingness' to advance a global framework curbing shipping's roughly 3% share of world emissions[^src1]. UCL analysis of country positions found 38 governments backing a Net-Zero Framework-style deal versus 17 opposed, with resistance concentrated among fossil-fuel-aligned and shipowner-reliant states such as the US, Russia, India and several Gulf nations[^src1]. Researchers warned that compromises being discussed, including a Brazil-proposed slower start, could increase overall emissions and undermine financial support meant for low-income countries during the transition[^src1]. Separate analysis by IMarEST found that even the most ambitious version of the framework on the table would fall short of the sector's 2030, 2040 and 2050 emissions targets, with only a stricter Pacific-proposed levy meeting those goals[^src1]. Further negotiating rounds are scheduled for late November and early December before a potentially final decision session begins December 4[^src1].
