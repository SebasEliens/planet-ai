ESA is preparing for the joint launch of two Earth observation satellites, FLEX and Copernicus Sentinel-3C, scheduled for 15 September from Kourou aboard the same rocket[^esa-flex-s3c-leop-2026]. Because the spacecraft will enter closely related orbits, ESOC is running side-by-side launch and early orbit phase (LEOP) simulations for two independent mission teams that must coordinate shared resources and respond jointly to anomalies[^esa-flex-s3c-leop-2026]. Flight Operations Director Franco Marchese noted that while ESA has launched paired satellites before, such as Herschel and Planck, this is the first time two fully separate teams must operate satellites in nearly the same orbit simultaneously[^esa-flex-s3c-leop-2026]. Rehearsals have included staggered signal acquisition, orbit-determination discrepancies, spacecraft anomalies and even a fire drill to test the teams' ability to maintain operations under combined stress[^esa-flex-s3c-leop-2026]. Sentinel-3C will extend Copernicus monitoring of oceans, land, ice and atmosphere, while FLEX is ESA's dedicated photosynthesis-monitoring mission[^esa-flex-s3c-leop-2026].
