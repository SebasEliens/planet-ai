A new paper tests whether large language models apply different standards of fairness to people from different countries by asking five LLMs to recommend compensation for a 60-minute appointment delay, varying only the stated country of the user[^arxiv-2610.18515]. Across 193 countries, the models consistently suggested higher compensation for European users and lower compensation for African users[^arxiv-2610.18515]. Comparing responses to GDP per capita (PPP), mean income, and a patience index, the authors found LLMs underestimate the value of time for people in Asia and South America[^arxiv-2610.18515]. The researchers argue this shows geographic bias is embedded in these models and could lead to unequal treatment when LLMs operate as personal assistants or agents[^arxiv-2610.18515].
