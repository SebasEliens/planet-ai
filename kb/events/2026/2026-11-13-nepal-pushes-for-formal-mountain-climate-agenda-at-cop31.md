Nepal's Minister of Agriculture, Forests and Environment, Gita Chaudhary, writes that Nepal has joined Bhutan and Kyrgyzstan in pushing COP31 to adopt a standalone agenda item recognizing the distinct climate threats facing mountain regions[^climatechangenews-nepal-mountain-agenda]. The proposal, now under discussion in COP presidency consultations in Antalya, also calls for a new Himalayan Climate Resilience Mechanism and seeks predictable, grant-based finance plus support for research and early warning systems[^climatechangenews-nepal-mountain-agenda]. The push follows the August 26, 2026 Rasuwa-Bhotekoshi glacial lake outburst flood, which killed more than 1,400 people, left over 5,200 missing, and is estimated to cost Nepal at least 10% of GDP to address[^climatechangenews-nepal-mountain-agenda]. The article notes 21 glacial lakes in Nepal are now classified as dangerous due to melting permafrost and ice, underscoring the risk of repeat disasters[^climatechangenews-nepal-mountain-agenda].
