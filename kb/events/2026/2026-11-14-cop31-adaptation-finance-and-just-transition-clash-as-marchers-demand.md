On day six of COP31 in Antalya, thousands of protesters marched through the city chanting against fossil fuels and demanding climate finance, a just transition mechanism, and reparations for loss and damage, alongside calls to protect Indigenous rights[^src1]. Negotiators meanwhile worked on a draft adaptation finance text that could lock in Africa's demand to triple adaptation finance to at least $120 billion a year by 2035, though an alternative option would instead launch a review process delaying the target[^src1]. A separate informal note establishing a new Just Transition Mechanism drew criticism from civil society for its limited ambition[^src1]. Türkiye and Australia, as COP31 co-presidents, convened a Saturday night stocktaking plenary meant to surface countries' key red lines as ministers arrive for the final negotiating push[^src1]. CAN International's Tasneem Essop stressed that "climate finance is the most important barrier to climate action in the Global South"[^src1].
