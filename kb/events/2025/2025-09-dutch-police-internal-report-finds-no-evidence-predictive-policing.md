An internal Dutch police report called the 'Kraai' report, dated September 2025 and obtained through freedom-of-information requests, found that the Crime Anticipation System (CAS) correctly predicted only one in fifty incidents in Amsterdam, far below earlier claims made by police researchers.[^algorithmwatch-2026] The report concluded that the algorithm's 'added value' was unclear, and police later admitted they could not prove CAS had reduced burglaries.[^algorithmwatch-2026] These findings led to the discontinuation of CAS in early 2026, after roughly a decade of nationwide use to flag crime 'hotspots' based on police records and socioeconomic data like income and household composition.[^algorithmwatch-2026] A 2023 Human Rights and AI Impact Assessment had already flagged concerns that CAS could affect the presumption of innocence, equal treatment, and the right to algorithmic explainability.[^algorithmwatch-2026]
